FLean nav + separate Inbox (fixes E's noise)

E's noise came from one surface doing two opposite jobs: permanent navigation AND the activity firehose. F splits them. Inbox is where the recency-sorted previews + governance events live — you go there to triage "what's new across everything." Chats is lean navigation: squads and DMs as identity-only rows (avatar + name + unread count, no preview text), and crucially only the selected squad expands to show its channels — so you never see more than one workspace's channel tree at a time. The Inbox tab carries the unread count so the firehose is one glance away without cluttering the nav.

Notification convention: a number means items directed at you where the count is actionable — DMs (BorrowLucid 2, Jane Doe 1) and squads with @mentions inside (Chones Party 3). A dot means ambient unread where the exact count is noise — broadcast channels (#announcements) and busy squads with nothing aimed at you (8004 Builders). The signal is "does this need me," not "how much is unread."

Identity & presence: the avatar top-right carries a status dot and opens the panel shown — keys in JetBrains Mono (they're technical data, the one place mono belongs), each with copy-to-clipboard, plus presence selection. Keeping identity in the top bar rather than the sidebar keeps it stable across Inbox / Chats / Commons, since it belongs to you, not to whichever surface you're in.

GSidebar owns navigation; Squads & DMs toggled

The fix for F's awkwardness: Inbox and Chats/Commons were never peers. Squads, DMs, Commons are places you go; Inbox is a view across them. Forcing both levels into one segmented control is why adding a second toggle underneath felt wrong. Here the sidebar owns all navigation — Inbox and Commons sit at the top as fixed destinations, then a Squads/DMs toggle switches your conversation list, with search beneath. The topbar drops to brand + identity only, so there's one nav column instead of two competing bars. Note the count on the inactive DMs segment: a toggle hides the other list, so its unread state must surface on the control itself or it goes invisible.

HIcon rail (lenses) + labeled sidebar

The Slack model, mapped to Pacto's actual hierarchy. The README's three levels — Network (Squad-of-Squads) → Squad → Channel — plus cross-cutting DMs and Commons, are hard to express in one flat list. So: the rail holds global lenses (Home / DMs / Activity / Commons) — each a destination with a homogeneous list, which is why the Squads-vs-DMs split needs no toggle. The sidebar shows the active lens's contents; in Home that's the current squad, with its Network named above it (all three levels visible at once) and its channels below. Identity + presence live at the bottom of the rail. This is the "combo of icons and sidebar" with the hierarchy made explicit.

IDiscord model — squad rail + categorized channels + Hat-grouped members

Discord's shape, Pacto's substance. The familiar four columns — squad rail, categorized channels, user panel bottom-left, chat — but rebuilt with Pacto's own parts: the coupon Add-channel, the Commons globe as the rail's discovery slot (not a stock compass), and channels that are governance-first. Two things you can't get from Discord: the member list groups by on-chain Hats (Squad Admin / Quartermaster / Crew, coloured by role) so it reads the squad's actual governance, and on-chain events post into the stream — see Pacto Gov's proposal result, stamped with its Sepolia network. One Sepolia network today, so there's no network switcher — the rail is squads, full stop.
