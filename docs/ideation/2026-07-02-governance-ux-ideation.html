Ideation: Friendly Governance UX for Pacto

Date: 2026-07-02

Topic: governance-ux

Mode: repo-grounded

Focus: Build a UX for governance that is easy and friendly for everyone to use without knowing the technical bits behind the smart contracts.

Grounding Context

Pacto is a Tauri v2 desktop app with a Svelte/TypeScript frontend and Rust backend. Governance is exposed inside a Squad/Network Dashboard via four tabs—Governance, Roles Tree, Treasury, Settings—plus a Deploy Launchpad. On-chain features require a Squad Sponsor first, then Pacto Gov (Nave Pirata: Hats + Safe), Squad Admin, or Safe.

The current UI surfaces dense contract jargon ("ERC-4337 gas pool," "Nave Pirata factory bundle," "Hats tree," "bytes32 role identifiers," "FULL vs PAUSE executor scopes") and lacks create-proposal, role-assignment, and plain-language onboarding UX. The product strategy is to combine Nostr/MLS private messaging with EVM/Aztec enforceable governance using a single Nostr-derived key, no KYC, and the Nave Pirata two-body democracy metaphor.

Key prior art includes Snapshot (gasless voting), Safe (shared-account multisig UX), Aragon App (no-code permissions), Loomio (cooperative deliberation), and credit-union digital voting (quorum meters, secret ballots). Persistent DAO problems include <10% voter turnout and friction between off-chain signaling and on-chain execution.

Topic Axes

  1. Deployment & setup — How to get from zero to a governable squad without expert knowledge.
  2. Proposal lifecycle — Create, vote, approve, and execute treasury decisions in plain language.
  3. Roles & permissions — Understand who is captain, crew, and who can do what.
  4. Treasury UX — View balances, propose spending, and understand approvals without Safe/multisig jargon.
  5. Dispute & mutiny — Replace a captain, handle conflicts, and recover from deadlock.

1. One-Click Default Deploy with Reversible Defaults

Deployment & setup Conf 90% Cx Low

Description: Collapse the sponsor → Pacto Gov → Safe deploy chain into a single 'Start this squad' action. The app autofills the captain from the embedded wallet, generates the metadata URI from the squad profile, and picks a random salt nonce. A visual progress checklist replaces the locked Launchpad cards, and an 'Advanced' toggle reveals all current inputs for review or editing. Every choice remains reversible later.

Basis: direct: DeployNavePirataWizard already defaults the captain from the embedded wallet but still exposes network, metadata URI, and salt nonce (src/components/parent/governance/DeployNavePirataWizard.svelte:2-109); LaunchpadModal enforces sponsor-before-gov order with ERC-4337 / Nave Pirata factory jargon (src/components/parent/governance/LaunchpadModal.svelte:36-54); PACTO_GOV.md describes the factory as an 'atomic one-shot deployment of Safe + hat tree + clones + wiring'. reasoned: Manual inputs that can be derived are user-hostile; the existence of a default means the wizard should be able to run without asking.

Rationale: Deployment is the top-of-funnel gate to every governance feature. One smooth setup unlocks proposals, roles, treasury, and mutiny forever after, matching the product's no-KYC, one-key premise.

Downsides: Hides chain and salt choices by default; advanced users must expand to verify. Requires robust error handling so a failed background step surfaces clearly rather than silently stalling.

2. Poll-to-Proposal Pipeline with Plain-Language Cards

Proposal lifecycle Conf 85% Cx Medium

Description: Add a 'Make it official' action on existing squad polls that converts the poll question and outcome into a treasury proposal with a human-readable title, purpose, amount, and recipient. The Governance tab renders proposals as payment slips—not 'Proposal #N target 0x…'—showing a clear checklist: Crew vote → Captain sign → Executed. The poll pattern in #announcements becomes the social front door to enforceable decisions.

Basis: direct: DashboardGovernanceTab has no create-proposal CTA and only renders vote buttons on existing proposals (src/components/parent/dashboard/DashboardGovernanceTab.svelte:1-50); TreasuryProposalCard shows only proposalId, yeas/nays, snapshot, deadline, and raw target address (src/components/parent/governance/TreasuryProposalCard.svelte:42-51); past learnings note polls are created as visible cards in #announcements and replicated over MLS (docs/dashboard/POLLS.md). external: Snapshot and Loomio separate off-chain signaling from on-chain execution; the friction between them is a known DAO failure mode.

Rationale: Users already vote in polls inside chat. Bridging that to enforceable treasury action removes the 'where do I create a proposal?' dead end and closes the signal-to-execution gap.

Downsides: Needs a way to store human-readable metadata off-chain (Nostr event or IPFS) and link it to the on-chain proposal. Must avoid making every poll into a binding proposal accidentally.

3. Pirate Metaphor + Roster-as-Roles

Roles & permissions Conf 90% Cx Medium

Description: Replace visible Hats/bytes32/Safe jargon with the canonical pirate-ship vocabulary: Captain, Crew, Quartermaster, Mutiny, Treasury. The primary role UI becomes the squad member roster: the captain taps a member and assigns a ship role, and the app translates that into the underlying Hats/bytes32 operations. The Hats tree becomes a secondary read-only 'org chart' for advanced users.

Basis: direct: DashboardRolesTreeTab shows a Hats tree explorer link and 'Untitled hat' fallback (src/components/parent/dashboard/DashboardRolesTreeTab.svelte:23-50); HatsTreeNode renders hat id and supply/maxSupply without explanation (src/components/parent/governance/HatsTreeNode.svelte:7-14); permissions-panel lists 'On-chain role identifiers' and 'FULL versus PAUSE executor scopes' (src/lib/dashboard/permissions-panel.ts:19-56); docs/pacto_ecosystem_research.md states the pirate-ship Captain/Crew model is canonical. external: Aragon App's no-code permission grants show roles can be assigned without touching contract code.

Rationale: The intended vocabulary already exists in the product docs; the UI should use it. One consistent metaphor makes every future screen—treasury, proposals, mutiny—self-explanatory without repeated onboarding.

Downsides: Engineers familiar with Hats Protocol may resist hiding contract terms. Must maintain a mapping table between UI terms and on-chain identifiers.

4. Unified Squad Wallet with Spend Composer and Safe-to-Spend Balance

Treasury UX Conf 90% Cx Medium

Description: Present all vaults as a single 'Squad Wallet' with one balance and a feed of pending spending requests. A 'Spend' button asks three questions: who gets it, how much, and why. The review screen shows the two-body approval chain ('Crew votes → Captain signs') and a live 'safe to spend' number that subtracts approved-but-not-yet-executed outflows. Raw Safe addresses and signer counts are available under an advanced disclosure.

Basis: direct: DashboardTreasuryTab lists vaults with raw addresses and external links (src/components/parent/dashboard/DashboardTreasuryTab.svelte:46-48); DeploySafeModal asks users to pick signers from members who have shared EVM addresses and warns about max signer counts (src/components/parent/governance/DeploySafeModal.svelte:10-272); PACTO_GOV.md states TreasuryAuthority enforces two-body democracy; proposal statuses include 'active_passed_crew' meaning funds are committed but not yet moved. external: Safe's queue-centric UX aims to hide M-of-N complexity; the ecosystem doc compares the treasury to a 'shared safe' with a two-person rule.

Rationale: Treasury is the most common governance action. Abstracting Safe/multisig mechanics into a shared account with a clear approval chain makes every future treasury interaction simpler and prevents accidental overcommitment.

Downsides: Aggregating balances across vaults must handle tokens and chains consistently. A unified balance could obscure which Safe holds which funds.

5. Mutiny as No-Confidence Chat Poll + Graceful Handoff

Dispute & mutiny Conf 85% Cx Medium

Description: Surface 'Replace Captain' as a visible, stigma-free safety feature in the Governance tab and squad chat. Any crew member can start a structured poll with a required reason and a 7-day window; the UI shows current crew support, the 51% threshold, and the timelock countdown. The captain can also initiate a 'Pass the torch' resignation plan that names a successor, with crew ratification. Mutiny becomes a responsible procedure, not a hidden escape hatch.

Basis: direct: PACTO_GOV.md describes MutinyModule as '51% crew vote can replace the captain (or captain can resign)' and the Quartermaster as a 'timelocked crew roster; admin of the crew hat'; docs/pacto_ecosystem_research.md describes mutiny as a formal, on-chain process; DashboardGovernanceTab has no mutiny action and only surfaces conflict through the after-the-fact 'captain_vetoed' status. external: Loomio's structured decision threads show how deliberation can handle conflict; aviation emergency protocols provide a model for formal, high-stakes procedures.

Rationale: Governance trust depends on credible recovery. If users cannot find mutiny until after a captain veto, the strongest safety mechanism is effectively absent. A normal handoff path also covers the common case of planned captain departure.

Downsides: Prominently surfacing mutiny could normalize conflict or create anxiety. Must ensure the poll cannot be gamed and that the reason is preserved for audit.

6. Private-by-Default Treasury Votes via Aztec

Treasury UX Conf 75% Cx High

Description: Use the Aztec privacy layer to make treasury votes private by default: members see their own ballot as a sealed envelope until the result is revealed, and the app explains this in plain language. The status rail shows 'Sealed votes' during the crew phase and 'Result revealed' after the captain signs, without exposing individual positions on-chain.

Basis: direct: docs/pacto_ecosystem_research.md states privacy is a core Pacto value, with Aztec/Noir for private voting and payments, and compares it to a sealed envelope the blockchain can verify without opening; the app already embeds Aztec in the integration architecture. reasoned: Public votes expose members' positions and treasury activity, which contradicts the product strategy for activists and dissidents.

Rationale: Delivers on the central promise of Pacto: enforceable rules without surveillance. It also differentiates Pacto from Snapshot/Tally, where public voting is the default.

Downsides: High engineering complexity; Aztec integration may not be production-ready. UX must explain why a private vote matters without scaring users.
